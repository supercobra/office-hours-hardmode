# Example session

**This transcript is fabricated.** It was written to illustrate the shape of the
interrupt and the directive. It is not a record of a real session. The founder,
the company, the customers, and every number in it are invented.

It is here because the format is easier to read than to describe. It is not
evidence that the skill works, and it should not be read as any.

A real graded transcript replaces this file the first time one exists.

---

**Claude:** Last time you said you'd send the one-page pricing memo to the two
enterprise leads, by August 4. The test was: both replied with a number or a
"no." Did you?

**Founder:** I sent one. The other I'm still polishing.

**Claude:** That's partial. The polishing is the tell — the memo isn't the
bottleneck, the answer is. What did the one who replied say?

**Founder:** He said $40k felt high for a pilot.

**Claude:** Good. That's the most real thing in this conversation. Hold it.
What did you want to work on today?

**Founder:** I want to redo onboarding. Activation is bad and I think the
first-run experience is confusing.

**Claude:** We're not doing onboarding today. Ten minutes ago you said "we're
raising to get to the next milestone" and that you've deferred your salary
since May. That decides whether onboarding matters — if you have eleven weeks
of runway, a better first-run experience ships after you're out of money.
Twenty minutes on runway, then onboarding. Yes?

**Founder:** ...yeah. Okay.

*[twenty minutes]*

**Claude:**

**DIRECTIVE:** Send the second pricing memo tomorrow at $18k, unpolished.

**BY:** August 14

**VERIFY:** The email is in your sent folder with a number in it.

**EXPECT:** He says yes or counters. Either one gets you a real price, which is
the input to whether you're raising or defaulting-alive.

**KILL:** If both enterprise leads go silent after seeing a number, you don't
have a pricing problem, you have a demand problem, and we start over.

Say the date back to me.

**Founder:** August 14.

---

On onboarding, since we skipped it: your activation number is probably fine and
your problem is that the people arriving aren't the people you built for. Look
at who activated, not who didn't.
